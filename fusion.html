Two traditions at a glance

Similarities and differences between Warli and Kalamkari
AspectWarliKalamkari
SurfaceMud walls, traditionally coated with red ochre or a cow-dung mixtureCotton (also silk), prepared before painting
MediumWhite paste made from riceNatural dyes such as indigo and madder
ToolsCrushed bamboo stick used as a brushBamboo pen (kalam) or carved wooden blocks
PaletteWhite on earthy grounds (brown, ochre, brick red)Plant-based dyes in colours such as navy, rust, ochre, green and black
MotifsCircle, triangle and square; human and animal bodies built from two triangles joined at the tipPersian-influenced vines and florals with Indian motifs such as parrots, lotuses and cartwheels
ThemesDaily life, farming, dance, celebrations, animals and natureMythological narratives (Srikalahasti); florals and geometric repeats (Machilipatnam)
RegionThane and Palghar districts, MaharashtraAndhra Pradesh: Srikalahasti (freehand pen) and Machilipatnam/Pedana (block printing)
GI registrationCertificate dated 31 March 2014Srikalahasti: April 2005. Machilipatnam: April 2008
